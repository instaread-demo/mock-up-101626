Adelaide Laneway Renamed in Honour of Hilltop Hoods

Hometown heroes and hip hop trio Hilltop Hoods officially have an Adelaide Laneway renamed in their honour.

Image: Ashlee Jones

Neil Griffiths
|
15.10.2026

Hometown heroes Hilltop Hoods officially have an Adelaide Laneway renamed in their honour.

Located in Adelaide’s West End, the street, formerly known as Clubhouse Lane, was unveiled at a special event today by Lord Mayor Dr Jane Lomax-Smith, which the trio attended.

“Once considered the Australian music industry’s underdogs, the Hoods have harnessed their talent and determination to not only top the charts but build a multi-generational fan base in their mosh pits,” the Lord Mayor said.

“Despite their global success, they have never forgotten their Adelaide roots. That’s why it’s fabulous to permanently recognise their achievements on the streets of the city – just a short walk from the venues where they started making music three decades ago.”

Hilltop Hoods Lane features two large-scale murals by renowned Adelaide artist Vans the Omega and is the sixth addition to the City of Adelaide’s City of Music Laneways trail. It joins tributes to South Australian music legends including Sia, Cold Chisel, No Fixed Address, Paul Kelly and The Angels.

President and CEO of Universal Music, Sean Warner, also made an appearance to present Hilltop Hoods with a plaque celebrating their single “1955” ft. Montaigne & Tom Thum, surpassing more than one million ARIA single sales and accredited 14 x Platinum.

“As pioneers whose impact reaches well past the boundaries of Australian hip-hop, Hilltop Hoods have left a permanent footprint on our national culture,” Warner said.

Australian Acts Take on New Birdie’s Brekkie Jingle

Macca’s has given Birdie a theme song, and Australian artists have now started putting their own spin on it.

Image: Supplied

Hameda Nafiz
|
11.09.2026

Created In Partnership with McDonald’s

Macca’s has given Birdie a theme song, and Australian artists have now started putting their own spin on it.

The original Birdie’s Brekkie jingle was released on Spotify on August 26th, drawing on ’00s pop and classic cartoon anthems. Built around synths and a catchy beat, it has the feel of a pop soundtrack, with a bright, upbeat sound at its centre.

The jingle has since made its way into the hands of Australian musicians and creators, with Jordan Anthony, Josie, Sidney, Overnight and Mashd N Kutcher among those taking it on. Their versions join the original on Birdie’s Brekkie Beats, alongside other covers and remixes from Aussie creators and fans.

Josie starts out somewhere considerably dreamier, before moving into country-bop territory and eventually landing in pop-rock. It begins almost like a completely different song before the original melody starts to emerge underneath it.

Jordan Anthony, meanwhile, slows things down. His version brings in more keys and a slower tempo, giving the jingle a softer, more aspirational feel than the upbeat original. The arrangement leaves a little more space around the melody.

Overnight takes things up a notch, speeding through the song with a much more energetic arrangement. There’s a bigger, more hyped-up quality to their version, which feels built to get you moving rather than easing you into the morning. And those are only a few of the versions that have emerged so far.
